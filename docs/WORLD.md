# DeBrief — World Bible v0.1

*The lore document for DeBrief. Chase's ruling: the lore is extremely important — this bible is
content, not flavour. Faction doctrine written here drives the simulation that generates
operations (DESIGN.md §6). Names and facts below stand until Chase renames or strikes them.*

## 1. The divergence — how America fell without a shot fired at it

The timeline splits in the late 1970s and arrives, by 1987, somewhere unrecognizable:

- **The Emergency (1979–81).** Economic collapse, energy shocks, and street violence let a
  disciplined, flag-wrapped movement — the **Vigilance Party** in its public face — sell the
  country a single product: order. Emergency powers were granted "temporarily," renewed
  annually, then renewed forever. The curfew in the city is temporary. It has been temporary
  for eleven years.
- **The Quiet Year (1983).** The courts were "streamlined," the press was "coordinated,"
  and the last election that mattered was held. Nothing burned. That is the horror of this
  world: it fell in good order, with paperwork, and most people were home for dinner.
- **The Settlement (1984– ).** The old federal agencies were absorbed, purged, or exiled.
  The Regime governs through **Administrators** — one per district — and rules the streets
  with the two instruments of DESIGN.md §3: its soldiery and its mechas.
- **The accelerated century.** The emergency state poured money into control technology a
  generation early: the walking machines, the listening networks, an arms program that put
  the HK USP into Agency hands years before its time (DESIGN.md §4 — the world bible keeps
  the receipt: the USP is a product of the Regime's own rearmament crash programs, which is
  why the exile NSA's standard sidearm is a gun the old world never issued).

## 2. The Regime

- **The name it uses:** the **Administration** — always "the Administration," as if there
  had never been another. Its ruling party is the Vigilance Party; its uniformed arm in the
  districts answers to the local Administrator.
- **The mark: three white bars on red.** Worn as the soldier's armband, painted on the
  mecha's shoulder. The bars stand for the regime's three founding pledges — **Order,
  Unity, Silence** — in that order, always in that order. Citizens read them as a warning;
  the Agency reads them as a signature: where the three bars are, the Administration has
  been, and has taken the trouble to say so.
- **Doctrine:** terror as administration. The soldiery does not merely occupy — it raids,
  reprisals, and makes examples, on a schedule the districts learn to fear like weather.
  The mechas are the same doctrine rendered in steel: not battlefield weapons (there is no
  battlefield) but instruments of presence — armoured arguments that resistance is arithmetic.
- **Its weakness (the game lives here):** the Administration is a bureaucracy. It files
  everything, trusts its own paperwork, and cannot imagine being read. An agency with the
  best ears in the world can live inside its filing system like a worm in an apple.

## 3. The NSA — a service in exile

- What the Settlement could not absorb, it scattered — and the listeners scattered best.
  The exile NSA is a remnant network: listening posts in friendly embassies that officially
  do not exist, dead-letter infrastructure older than the Regime, and a handful of field
  assets inside the occupation. It kept its ears when it lost its country.
- **Control** is not a person so much as a voice and a protocol — the handler on the
  scrambled line, the man in the debrief room (§5 of the design). Whether Control is one
  officer or a duty desk is a question the series answers late, if ever.
- The Agency's war is archival: it intends to out-remember the Regime. Every op files
  evidence — names, orders, signatures — toward a reckoning the file calls **the Archive**.
  This is why the debrief matters: the player is not just surviving the occupation, he is
  building the case against it, one operation at a time.

## 4. The city — Greyport

- **Greyport** (working name — an American Atlantic port): dockyards and container yards
  under watchtowers, a market arcade that never entirely closes, bridge checkpoints, a
  financial district gone grey and quiet, tenement districts where the curfew is a rumor
  the poor can't afford to believe. The Administration's designation: **Greyport Special
  Administrative District**.
- A city of spies because everyone needs it to be: the port is where the world's secrets
  change hands — couriers on the night ferries, defectors in the safe hotels, dead drops
  in the arcades. Foreign stations (the old allies and the old enemies alike) keep offices
  the Administration tolerates because it reads their mail.
- The districts are the dungeon (DESIGN.md §4): tall buildings and walls, courtyards as
  rooms, rooftops as the upper floor, the grapple's street network overhead.

## 5. The mecha program

- **Walkers, not tanks:** the Regime's machines are bipedal because the doctrine is
  presence, not war — a walker can climb a dockyard stair, stand in a plaza like a statue
  of the state, and look down. Patrol frames work the dockyards; siege frames hold the
  bridges. The concept art (`concept-art/enemy-mecha.jpg`) is a patrol frame: grey plate
  over black machinery, cannon arm, one red eye.
- They are crewed machines in the early marks and increasingly automated in the later
  ones — the timeline of the marks is a story the Archive is assembling, and it does not
  like where the arithmetic points.
- In play (DESIGN.md §3): weather, not bosses — sensors, arcs, armour; blind them,
  mislead them, drop the city on them. Never duel one.

## 6. Tone laws

- The occupation is bureaucratic before it is brutal; the brutality is what the paperwork
  authorizes. Horror here is a stamp, a signature, a red light on a recorder.
- Nobody in this world thinks they are the villain of it. The Regime's soldiers believe in
  Order the way the Agency believes in the Archive. The game believes both of them, and
  lets the record argue.
- 1987 is period-true except where the divergence is documented. Every anachronism in the
  world must have a receipt in this bible — that discipline is the lore's credibility.

## 7. Scale (Chase's directive, 2026-10-29)

- **Greyport is BIG.** Not a block with a skyline painted behind it — a real
  city: multiple districts (the dockyard, the old town, the Administration
  quarter, the market streets, the temple ground), the checkpoint crossings
  between them, and the rooftops as a second city laid over the first.
  Slice 1's block is not a sample of the city; it is the city's TILE — the
  generative pattern every district is a variation of.
- **Big means dense, not sprawling.** The founding line stands: a city like
  a dungeon. Acreage the player crosses without a decision is not size,
  it is dilution. Every district is a dungeon floor with its own light,
  its own patrol economy, its own way of being watched.
- **The engine's arithmetic already allows it:** the block is procedural
  and asset-free, the renderer tiles and culls, the governor prices
  distance. The city exists whole as DATA — every street, lamp, and door
  in the Archive's model — and becomes geometry only around the operator.
  An op witnesses one district deeply; a career walks the whole map.
- **Persistence is what makes big mean something:** the city remembers at
  city scale — welded mecha damage, a closed temple, a checkpoint that
  learned your face. A big city that forgot would just be a large one.
